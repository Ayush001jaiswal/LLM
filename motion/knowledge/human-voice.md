# Writing in a natural, specific voice

## Why generic wording hurts
Recruiters read many resumes, and generic phrases such as "results-driven professional", "passionate team player" and "proven track record" tell them nothing. Specific facts about what you did, with which tools, and for whom, are more believable and more memorable.

## Plain words
Prefer simple, precise verbs: built, wrote, fixed, led, cut, moved, taught, planned. Avoid inflated words such as "leveraged", "utilized", "spearheaded", "robust", "seamless" and "cutting-edge" when a plain word says the same thing.

## Vary the structure
Do not start every bullet with the same verb or use the same sentence shape every time. Mix short and longer bullets. Put the most important result first in each bullet.

## Use your own details
Use the real names of the tools, systems, teams and customers you worked with. Keep the terms you actually use at work. Details that only you could know make a resume read as yours.

## Summaries
A good summary is two or three lines with your role, your strongest skills and the kind of problems you solve. Skip empty claims about being hard-working or a fast learner.

## Check your own text
Read the resume aloud. If a sentence could appear in anyone's resume, replace it with something specific. Fix spelling and grammar, and have another person read it before you send it.
