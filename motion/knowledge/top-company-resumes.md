# Resumes for large technology companies

## What large companies tend to look for
Recruiters at large technology companies usually read a resume for under a minute. They look for relevant experience, clear impact, scale, and ownership. Impact means what changed because of your work. Scale means how many users, requests, records, dollars or people were involved. Ownership means what you personally designed, built or led. A resume cannot create experience, but it can make real experience easy to see.

## Impact statements
A widely used pattern is: accomplished [outcome], as measured by [metric], by doing [action]. For example: "Reduced checkout errors by [X%] by redesigning input validation". Use only outcomes and metrics that are true. If the exact number is confidential or unknown, describe the scope in plain words instead of guessing.

## Length and layout
Use one page unless you have many years of relevant experience. Use a single column, standard headings and plain text. Put the most relevant experience first and trim older or unrelated roles to one or two lines.

## Technical roles
Name the technologies you used inside the bullets where you used them, and link to code, a portfolio or a published project when you can. Projects and open-source work help people with little industry experience, but only if you can explain every detail in an interview.

## Honesty
Large companies check references and test the skills listed on a resume during interviews. Never list a skill, title, date, employer or result that is not true. A short, accurate resume is better than a long, exaggerated one.

## Referrals and fit
Strong candidates are often found through referrals and direct outreach as well as applications. A well-formatted resume is necessary but is not a guarantee of an interview, because roles are competitive and decisions depend on experience, role fit and timing.
