# Resume sections and what to include

## Summary
A summary is two or three lines at the top that state the role, the main strengths and the area of expertise, tailored to the target job. Avoid clichés such as "hard-working", "passionate" and "team player". Skip a generic objective statement. Freshers can use a short summary naming their degree, key skills and the kind of role they want.

## Skills
Group skills by category, for example Languages, Frameworks, Databases, Tools and Cloud. List only skills you can discuss in an interview. Put the skills that match the job description first. Do not rate skills with bars or stars.

## Experience
List jobs in reverse chronological order, newest first. Each job shows title, company, location and dates, followed by achievement-focused bullets. Internships and relevant part-time roles belong here too.

## Education
Show degree, field, institution and years. Add CGPA or percentage if it is strong or if you are a fresher. Experienced candidates keep this section short.

## Projects
Freshers and career changers should include two or three projects. For each, give the name, the technologies used, and two or three bullets about what was built and the outcome. A link to code or a live demo helps.

## Certifications and awards
Include only relevant certifications with the issuer and year. List notable awards, rankings and hackathon results briefly.

## What to leave out
Most recruiters do not need a photo, date of birth, marital status, father's name, religion, a full home address, a "Declaration" paragraph, or "References available on request". Many employers prefer not to receive personal details like these. Leave out hobbies unless they relate to the role. Do not include salary expectations.

## Order of sections
Experienced candidates: header, summary, skills, experience, projects, education, certifications. Freshers: header, summary, education, skills, projects, internships, certifications.
