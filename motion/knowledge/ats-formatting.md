# ATS resume formatting

## How applicant tracking systems work
An applicant tracking system (ATS) stores resumes, parses them into fields such as name, contact details, jobs, education and skills, and lets recruiters search and filter them. Most systems do not automatically reject resumes by score; a recruiter usually reads the shortlisted ones. The real risk is a parsing error: a field that is missing or garbled makes the candidate harder to find in search.

## Layout rules
Use a single column. Avoid tables, text boxes, multiple columns, headers and footers, images, icons, charts and skill-rating bars, because parsers often skip or scramble them. Put the name and contact details in the main body of the page, not in a header. Use a standard font such as Arial, Calibri or Times New Roman at 10 to 12 pt, and plain round bullet points.

## Section headings
Use standard headings that parsers recognise: Summary, Skills, Experience, Education, Projects, Certifications. Creative headings such as "My Journey" or "What I Bring" may not be recognised. Experienced candidates put Experience before Education. Freshers and recent graduates put Education before Experience and Projects.

## File type
Submit a DOCX or a text-based PDF unless the job posting says otherwise. A PDF must contain real selectable text; a scanned or exported image of a resume cannot be read. Name the file clearly, for example FirstName_LastName_Resume.

## Dates and contact details
Use one date format everywhere, for example "Jan 2022 – Present". Every job shows the job title, company, location and dates. The header has the full name, email, phone number, city, and a LinkedIn or portfolio link.

## Length
Use one page for freshers and for people with up to about 10 years of experience. Use at most two pages for senior candidates. Do not shrink the font below 10 pt to force a fit; cut the least relevant content instead.

## Keywords
Match the wording of the job description when it truthfully describes your experience, because recruiters search by keyword. Write acronyms in full once, for example "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)". Mention important skills both in the Skills section and inside experience bullets where they were used. Never stuff keywords or hide white text; recruiters and systems penalise it.
