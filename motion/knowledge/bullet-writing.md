# Writing strong resume bullet points

## Bullet structure
A strong bullet follows this shape: action verb, what you did, how or with what, and the result. Example: "Reduced page load time by [X%] by adding caching with Redis". Keep each bullet to one or two lines, about 8 to 25 words. Put the most important and most relevant bullets first under each job.

## Strong action verbs
Start with a specific verb. Building and technical work: Built, Designed, Developed, Implemented, Automated, Migrated, Optimized, Integrated, Deployed. Leadership: Led, Managed, Mentored, Coordinated, Directed, Organized. Results: Improved, Reduced, Increased, Delivered, Launched, Streamlined, Achieved. Analysis: Analyzed, Evaluated, Investigated, Forecasted, Identified.

## Tense
Use past tense for past roles and present tense for the current role. Keep the tense the same within one job.

## Weak phrases to avoid
Avoid "Responsible for", "Worked on", "Helped with", "Duties included", "Involved in" and "Participated in". They describe a duty, not an action or result. Rewrite by naming what you actually did. Example: "Responsible for testing the application" becomes "Tested the application and reported defects to developers". Do not add facts that were not in the original bullet.

## Showing results without inventing numbers
Recruiters respond to measurable results: how many, how often, how much faster, how large, how much saved. Only use numbers that are true and that you can explain in an interview. If you do not know a number, describe the scope or the outcome in plain words, for example "for a payment system used by several merchants", only when that is accurate. Never invent percentages, revenue figures, team sizes or tools.

## Number of bullets
Use 3 to 5 bullets for recent or relevant jobs and 1 to 2 for older or less relevant ones. Merge duplicate bullets. Remove tasks that every person in the role does and that add no information.

## Common mistakes
Starting every bullet with the same verb, writing long paragraphs, listing only tasks without outcomes, using first person ("I"), mixing tenses, and adding tools or skills that were not actually used.
